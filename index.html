About

I am Nikolaos Dionelis, PhD, a Principal Research Scientist III at the University of Alabama in Huntsville (UAH), working at NASA Marshall Space Flight Center in the Office of Data Science and Informatics (ODSI), within the IMPACT AI for Science team. I devise, implement, and run large-scale multi-modal AI foundation models for science, implementing NASA SMD’s AI-for-Science strategy of five science foundation models supported by the INDUS large language model, released openly on Hugging Face.

My methodological signature comes from a decade in signal processing: I bring wavelets (DWT, DT-CWT), frequency-domain analysis, and multi-scale hierarchy into discrete image tokenization (FSQ/VQ), JEPA world models, diffusion and flow matching, and 4M-style multi-modal pretraining — so that foundation models keep the high-frequency content of scientific imagery instead of smoothing it away. I run this work end-to-end on NASA high-performance computing: multi-node, multi-GPU A100 pretraining, ablation, and evaluation at scale.

Before Huntsville, I was an Internal Research Fellow at the European Space Agency’s Φ-lab (ESRIN, Italy), a four-year postdoctoral Research Associate in machine learning at the University of Edinburgh / UDRC, and earned my PhD at Imperial College London on modulation-domain Kalman filtering for speech enhancement, supported by an EPSRC Doctoral Training Award.

Research

Three celestial domains, one method: keep the physics in the pixels. Frequency-aware tokenization and generative pretraining for scientific imagery, from the solar corona to the lunar surface to Earth’s land cover.

☉ Sun · Heliophysics Foundation Model

Forecasting the Sun days ahead

Core methods and model runs for the Heliophysics Multi-Modal FM, extending the NASA–IBM Surya lineage — pre-trained on nine years of Solar Dynamics Observatory data (AIA 8 channels + HMI 5 channels). Downstream: forecasting strong solar flares (class X) and coronal mass ejections days in advance, protecting crewed spaceflight, satellites, power grids, and aviation; segmentation of coronal holes, solar wind, and coronal loops. Co-author of “Beyond 3 Million Tokens”, submitted to NeurIPS 2026.

SDO AIA + HMI · 13 channels NSF NAIRR · 650,000 GPU-hours NeurIPS 2026 · under review
☾ Moon · Lunar Foundation Model

Tokenizers that respect lunar terrain

First-author lead on tokenization for the Lunar FM (LRO WAC & NAC imagery + DTM elevation/slope/aspect). Devised novel DWT- and DT-CWT-based spectral wavelet tokenizers and a dual-codebook FSQ tokenizer (morphology / amplitude streams, wavelet-packet decomposition, SpectFormer backbone); integrated them as frozen tokenizers into 4M/TerraMind-style multi-modal pretraining, achieving ~24% validation-loss reduction versus the spatial baseline at matched compute. Downstream: crater detection and age estimation, Artemis landing-site identification, and cave, pit, volcano, and boulder detection on the Moon and Mars.

LRO WAC · NAC · DTM WACV 2027 · first author · under review bidirectional WAC VIS ↔ DTM generation
⊕ Earth · Observation Foundation Models

Generative multimodality for Earth observation

Co-author of TerraMind (ICCV 2025), large-scale generative multimodality for EO with IBM Research. At ESA Φ-lab I led the design and benchmarking of the PhilEO Bench, devised the CARE confidence-aware regression model (IJCNN 2025), and scaled PhilEO pretraining to the MajorTOM dataset (first-author scaling-laws study). At NASA, I build HLS data pipelines aligned with the Prithvi geospatial FM family — the first AI geospatial foundation model demonstrated in orbit — for flood mapping, fire-scar identification, and crop classification.
